# Scroll and HeaderFade

Part of the closed set of components: [DESIGN.md, "Components"](../../DESIGN.md#components) has the rules every component keeps, its variant words and the fixed measures the geometry check holds it to.

## `Scroll`

Rust `layout::scroll`; QML `Scroll`. Its mockup page is the source of truth, with `HeaderFade`: Molecules, Scroll and HeaderFade, in `design/mockups/`.

A page's content taller than its room. The thumb is 4 wide (`scrollbar`), on the right edge of its box, a pill in the outline variant (the outline while the pointer is on it), floating over the content so it takes no width: content that runs to the edge leaves 16 on its right. A scroll clips what it scrolls, a focus glow included, only at an edge you can see. A page's or a panel's scroll has no edge of its own, so it reaches past its box into the room around it, as far as a glow reaches (`focus.far`, 20), and above and below up to the part of the page there (`room_above`, `room_below`, `roomAbove`, `roomBelow`: Kōbō's header and footer, the wallpaper picker's tabs), never less than a glow's reach, with its content that far in: the content lines up with what is around it, a resting control at its edge keeps its whole glow, content scrolled past its box shows in that room and stops at the part beside it, and the panel's or window's own edge cuts whatever passes it. In iced, whose content leaves 16 on its right for the thumb, it reaches the 4 of the glow past that room on its right, so the thumb keeps its place and never takes the pointer from the content; in QML it reaches the glow there too, and the thumb floats over the content's last 4. A scroll with an edge of its own (a list's well, a log, a menu) clips at that edge.

## `HeaderFade`

QML `HeaderFade`; *QML only: iced's scrolls have no fade yet, and it is on the QML specimen's "Shell only" page, which the parity check leaves out.*

The gap under a panel's header (or the Command menu's field) and the top of the list or form that scrolls beneath it: content scrolled up is faded out, not painted over. While the content is away from its top (`scrolled`) the scroll's view is masked: what passes over the gap (`gap`, a panel's step of 16, or the Command menu's padding under its field) is hidden, and it fades back in over the next 16, instead of being cut flat under the header's words. A mask and not a fill, so everything under the header goes the same, a lit control's tube and its glow included, across the view's whole reach, and the panel's glass shows through as it does round the header, with no band of opaque `surface` over it. It fades in and out over `micro`, so at rest a focused first row keeps its whole glow. The thumb, over the content, is never masked. The system monitor's processes, the notifications panel and the Command menu's forms. *Not built yet: QML's `HeaderFade` paints a gradient of opaque `surface` over the list, which a lit card's glow at the list's reach passes beside and which reads as a slab on the panel's glass (at `opacity.frame`). It needs to mask its scroll instead: the list as a layer (`layer.enabled`) under a `MultiEffect` mask, or a `ShaderEffectSource` and an alpha mask of the same gradient, transparent over the gap and opaque `space.s16` below it, its strength following `scrolled`.*

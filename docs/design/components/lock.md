# Lock

Part of the closed set of components: [DESIGN.md, "Components"](../../DESIGN.md#components) has the rules every component keeps, its variant words and the fixed measures the geometry check holds it to.

## `Lock`

QML `Lock`, a template of the shell's alone: only the shell locks, so it is in `KubaOS.Components` only, with its section on the QML specimen's "Shell only" page. Its mockup page is the source of truth: Templates, Lock, in `design/mockups/`. *Not built yet: `modules/lock/LockSurface.qml` draws the lock as a column over a band of `opacity.backdrop`, with the clock in `clock` and the `size.password` field under it, in the light or dark scheme of the mode.*

The lock screen. It has two phases, rest and awake, and always draws in the night scheme matugen derives from its wallpaper (`scheme-fidelity` in dark, as `dot-theme` makes it, with its derived roles), whatever the light or dark mode, so it looks the same in both.

**At rest** it is the wallpaper and its clock, and nothing else: no bar, no field. Every screen of a setup shows rest, each with its own clock.

- **The clock** is the digits in Geist Light, `type.clock_rest`, in the wallpaper's own colour, sized by the screen, not fixed: on a wide screen `scale.lock_clock` (0.3556) of its height, at most `scale.lock_clock_width` (0.2) of its width, so 384 on 1920 by 1080, 512 on 2560 by 1440, 768 on 3840 by 2160, 256 on 1280 by 720, and 384 on 1920 by 1200, where the width limits it; on a portrait screen sized once from the width, the widest time it can show (00:00 in Geist's proportional figures) filling the width less `space.s32` either side, 348 on 1080 by 1920, so no time leaves the screen and the size never changes as the minutes turn; a narrower time is drawn narrower, as on a wide screen (21:47 is 834 wide there). `type.clock_rest`'s 384 is what the rule gives on a 1080 tall screen, the reference the rest is drawn at; each size lands on whole pixels. Above it, the kicker line, its size `scale.lock_date` (0.0521) of the clock's and its baseline `scale.lock_date_gap` (0.1042) of it above the digits' cap tops, measured as the eye does, so the kicker's leading and descenders don't count (20 and 40 beside the 384 clock); on a portrait screen bigger and higher, `scale.lock_date_portrait` (0.115) and `scale.lock_date_gap_portrait` (0.25), 40 and 87 beside 348; never under `size.lock_date_least` (12). A kicker wider than the screen less `space.s32` either side shrinks to fit, to that least at most. It holds where and which day in `clock_place` (東京・月曜日, M PLUS 1), then ` · ` and the date in `clock_date` (Geist), both medium on a line 1.2 times their size (20 on a 24 line, medium, in one line. The digits' box is their cap height down to their baseline. The kicker and the digits are each centred on the clock's middle, on whole device pixels, the odd one after. With a 12-hour clock the digits carry no meridiem; it takes its own slot at the kicker's end (· PM), so the digits keep one size. The figures are Geist's own, proportional, so the digits' box takes each time's own width and stays centred on the clock's middle as a minute changes it.
- **The digits' colour** is a light tint of the picture behind them, worked out from the picture as the shell loads it, so they read as part of it rather than as words laid on it. Of the picture's pixels under the digits' box, each pixel's OKLCH hue counts by its chroma squared, so a sky's glow and a street's signs decide it and grey haze and dark walls barely do; the chroma is theirs, weighted the same way. That hue, at that chroma times 1.2 and at lightness 0.84, fitted into sRGB by lowering its chroma alone, is the colour (Shinjuku's pink horizon gives a pale pink, Tokyo Tower's blue hour a pale blue, Shibuya's lit storefronts a gold). The digits hold no contrast of their own and nothing shades them, a clock in front of its picture (no cut-out, or depth off) included; until the picture has arrived they are `on_surface`. The kicker takes the same colour, its date at `opacity.clock_date` (0.85) of it, so where and which day lead the line.
- **The layers.** A wallpaper with depth ships two pictures beside it, each its size: the cut-out, its city cut out as an alpha picture, and the plate, the picture with the city painted out (the cut-out's alpha grown by 12 px, filled by LaMa) and transparent everywhere else. The lock draws, from the back: the picture, the plate over it, the clock, then the city, which is the picture itself drawn through the cut-out's alpha, so its pixels are the picture's own and plate, picture and city at one scale are the picture exactly. A picture without depth is the picture, then the clock.
- **The depth.** At rest the picture and its plate stand pushed in to `scale.push` (1.025) and the city in front of them further, to `scale.near` (1.05), all round the middle of the screen's bottom edge, where the city stands, so the city stands off the sky. Growing from the bottom, its roofs only ever grow over the sky; what it uncovers of the picture behind is a band along the sides of its towers that face the middle, at most about 20 logical pixels at the screen's far corners, and the plate fills it. Only these thin bands of the plate are ever seen, so a plate that is a smear under a skyline (Shinjuku's, whose cut-out is the whole city) never shows. It holds still: nothing redraws while the lock sits there, and awake keeps it under the blur. A picture without depth pushes in alone, to `scale.push`, on the same origin; the desktop's picture is the same one at 1:1.
- **Its place** is the screen's, the same on every wallpaper, yours included: centred, the digits' baseline `scale.lock_clock_base` (0.5024) of the screen's height down on a wide screen, 543 on 1920 by 1080, and `scale.lock_clock_base_portrait` (0.40) down on a portrait one, 768 on 1080 by 1920. It holds still as the picture pushes in. Nothing about it is set per picture: between wallpapers only the digits' tint and the city's clipping differ.
- **The city**, where the wallpaper has depth, stands in front of the clock, so the towers stand in front of the digits: where its cut-out happens to cross the digits it clips them, where it doesn't it doesn't. A picture without one has the clock in front of it.
- **The kicker's halo** is all that stands it off the picture, and no shape: round each letter two text shadows of `surface`, a tight one blurred by `size.halo_edge` (1) at `opacity.halo_edge` (0.50) that holds the strokes' edges and a soft one blurred by `size.halo` (6) at `opacity.halo` (0.35) round the word. They follow the glyphs, so no capsule or band shows, and they are the same on every wallpaper, never worked out from the picture: the clock matching its wallpaper matters more than the kicker's contrast. Measured round each letter at rest, the kicker holds about 2.5:1 on Shinjuku's pink sky, 4.4 to 5.4:1 on Tokyo Tower's blue hour, and on Shibuya 12:1 for the place over a dark awning while the date crosses lit signs, 3.8:1 at its middle and under 2:1 at its brightest. The digits have no halo, with a cut-out or without.

**Awake** is the screen with the pointer only; the others stay at rest.

- **No frame and no card.** Nothing closes in round the picture. The desktop's bar stays hidden while it is locked, and slides down from above the screen, carrying its items, only while unlocking.
- **The picture** and its city blur to `size.lock_blur` (8) and dim under `scrim` at `opacity.panel_scrim`, as a panel's desktop does; the blur holds its edges, so it reaches the screen's. The clock stands between them and in front of the dim, so the city dims as the picture does (scrim is black, so the city's brightness drops by as much) and the clock does not.
- **The clock** has no awake size or place of its own. Waking takes it `scale.clock_awake` (0.2) of the way along its flight to the bar's clock, measured along the path, not in time: its size, its place and its colour each move that far from the rest clock's toward the bar's time, together. So it stays big (about 0.8 of `type.clock_rest`), a little higher and drawn a little toward the middle of the screen, above the dim and still behind the city, which stays, blurred and dimmed with the picture, and cuts the digits where it stands in front of them as at rest. Its digits are a fifth of the way from the picture's tint to `on_surface`, so they keep most of the tint. The kicker scales with the clock as it is drawn now, its size and its gap the clock's shares of its current size, so awake it is a fifth of the way smaller too (16 beside the 384 clock), never under `size.lock_date_least`, and it goes on scaling with the clock along the flight until it fades; it stays centred on the clock's middle on whole device pixels, its colours a fifth of the way from the tint to its own roles (where and which day toward `primary`, the date toward `on_surface_variant` at full opacity), and its halo stays. Unlocking flies the remaining four fifths from there.
- **The field** stands alone at the middle of the bottom edge, with no card under it: its room is a card's content's, `size.panel` (392) wide with a PanelPage's padding (`space.s16` at its top and foot, the container's padding at its sides), `size.gap` above the screen's bottom, so it stands where a card would hold it, and the glass's shadow round it lifts it off any picture. It is the md `Field` for the password: `lock` leads it in the field's icon colour, Password is its placeholder, and it is lit while the keyboard is in it. The field's trailing mark is `fingerprint` in `primary`, the same `type.body_large` icon as `lock` and `space.s12` in from the field's end as `lock` is from its start; on a machine without a fingerprint reader the field has none. Enter and Esc have no hints.
- No avatar, no initial, no emoji: only KubaOS Symbols.

States: checking a password or a finger, the field's symbol is `hourglass_top` and its placeholder Checking…, and the reader's mark stays `primary`; a wrong password, the field shakes, empties and turns Invalid, its tube `error`, and its placeholder says what went wrong (Wrong password) until the next key; an unknown finger, the reader's mark turns `error`, shaking alone, and the field's placeholder says what went wrong (Finger not recognised), the field staying as it was, unshaken, since the password wasn't wrong, until the next key or touch; at rest it wakes the lock, which arrives with the miss already shown. A known finger unlocks: awake, the reader's mark turns into `lock_open` and the lock unlocks as with the password; at rest, the clock goes straight into the bar.

**The desktop's still.** The lock and the desktop are one surface. The session locks in the first frame of Super+L, and the lock captures a still of the desktop then; locking and unlocking play on that still, drawn inside the lock's own surface: the windows, tiled by the desktop's gap at a window's corner, and the bar with its items, among them the bar's clock (the ClockPill, `docs/design/components/bar.md`: the weekday's seal, the short date, the time on the bar's middle, in the bar's row, and the weather; the time in `body`, `on_surface`, centred on whole device pixels in a slot as wide as the widest time its format can show: every time of the day measured on the font as the shell loads it, 00:00 to 23:59, or 1:00 to 12:59 for a 12-hour clock, since Geist's figures are proportional and no one digit is widest in every place. So the pill keeps its width as the minutes turn). The clock flies between the bar's time and its own place, above everything while it flies and behind the bar once it stands, and behind the city throughout: while it flies above everything the city's alpha, where the city stands as it pushes, is taken out of its digits, so the city cuts it exactly as it does when the clock stands behind it, and it never crosses the city; its digits the picture's tint at rest, toward `on_surface` as it flies, landing in `on_surface`, the colour of the bar's time at rest. It lands on the bar's time on whole device pixels, its left edge, its width, its baseline and `type.body`'s size. Geist Light's figures are a little narrower than Regular's, so as it lands its letters spread by what the bar's time is wider, an even share after each: it lands as wide as the bar's time, each figure within a fifth of a logical pixel of the bar's. The two are one family and differ only in weight, Light and Regular, so the handover is the bar's Regular fading in over a `micro` on top of the landed Light, which then goes, and leaving is the same backwards: the strokes thicken or thin in place and nothing else moves. The session unlocks as the unlocking ends, and the real windows take the still's place.

Motion:

- **Locking (Super+L):** from the first frame the lock draws the plate and the city over the picture, which is the desktop's picture exactly, the plate unseen until the push begins. The windows fade and shrink to `scale.behind` (0.96) round the middle of the screen over `medium` on `emphasized_accel`, and on the same `medium` and curve the bar slides straight up off the screen, carrying its items. The bar's time hands over to the lock's clock where it stands over `micro`, and over the same `micro` the seal, the date and the weather round the time fade; the pill, its time left empty, rides up with the bar: the clock leaves the pill as the pill leaves the screen, the one fast and the other gathering speed, so they part at once. From `micro` on the clock grows out of the bar to `type.clock_rest` at its rest place over `long` on `emphasized_decel`, above the windows and behind the city, its digits taking the picture's tint as it goes, the kicker fading in above it over `short` from `micro` and `medium` on. From `micro` on too, with the clock's flight, over `long` on `emphasized_decel`, the picture and its plate push in from 1:1 to `scale.push` and the city to `scale.near`, the plate's opacity following the push, so the city has left the picture's own outline by the time the plate is all there. The clock lands as the push does, and drops behind the bar between the picture and its city, which changes nothing on screen. With no depth the picture pushes in alone and the clock drops behind nothing. A lid close or an idle suspend skips the motion and goes straight to rest. Until the picture has arrived the screen is `surface_container_lowest`.
- **Waking:** the picture and its city blur over `long` on `emphasized_decel`, the dim comes over the picture and the city over `medium` on `standard`, the clock takes the first `scale.clock_awake` of its flight to the bar over `long` on `emphasized_decel`, its size, its place and its colour together on whole device pixels, still behind the city, its kicker shrinking with it, and the field rises `space.s8` into place as it fades in, over `long` on `emphasized_decel`. The push holds.
- **Going back to rest:** the field sinks `space.s8` as it fades, over `medium` on `emphasized_accel`; the dim lifts over `medium` on `standard`, the blur clears over `long` on `standard`, and the clock goes back along its flight to its rest place, size and tint over `long` on `emphasized_decel`. The field empties.
- **Unlocking with the password,** from awake, about 0.8 s in all: the field's `lock` turns to `lock_open` in `primary` over `short`. The field sinks as it fades, over `medium` on `emphasized_accel`, from `short` on. From `short` on too, the clock rises above everything, still cut by the city, and flies the rest of its flight, from where waking left it into the bar's clock, over `long` on `emphasized_decel`: its size, its place and its colour go on from the awake step with no jump. Its kicker fades over `short`, while the bar slides down from above the screen, carrying its items, on the same `long` and curve. The pill arrives with its time empty and nothing round it yet, and the clock lands in it as both settle; once it has landed it hands over to the bar's time over `micro`, and the seal, the date and the weather fade in round the time over `short`. From `medium` on the picture's blur and dim clear to the desktop's wallpaper and the windows settle in from `scale.behind`, over `long`, the picture and its plate easing back from `scale.push` and the city from `scale.near` to the desktop's 1:1 with them, on `emphasized_decel`, the plate fading with the push, so the last frame is the desktop's picture. The kicker fades as it goes, at the clock's size then.
- **Unlocking with the fingerprint, awake,** a known finger after an unknown one, the reader's mark in `error` and the placeholder saying Finger not recognised: over `short` on `standard` the mark eases back to `primary` and turns into `lock_open`, as the field's `lock` does after the right password: the `fingerprint` fades as a `lock_open` in `primary` laid over it fades in. The placeholder clears over the same `short`. The rest is unlocking with the password, from its `short` on: the field sinks, the clock flies into the bar as the bar slides in, and the picture eases back from the push to the desktop's.
- **Unlocking with the fingerprint,** from rest: the kicker fades over `micro`, and the clock rises above everything at once, still behind the city, which cuts its digits as they come up out from behind it, and the rest clock flies straight from `type.clock_rest` into the bar's clock over `long` on `emphasized_decel`, its digits going from the picture's tint to `on_surface`, and hands over to the bar's time over `micro` once it has landed, the seal, the date and the weather then fading in round the time over `short`. The bar slides down from above the screen carrying its items, the locking's slide reversed, on the same curve, so the clock lands in the pill as both settle, and from `short` on the windows settle in from `scale.behind` over `long`, the picture, its plate and its city easing back to the desktop's 1:1 with them on `emphasized_decel`. The kicker fades as it goes, at the clock's size then.
- **A wrong password** shakes the field: `space.s12`, back `space.s8`, `space.s4`, each over `micro` on `standard`, then home over `short` on `standard_decel`.
- **A finger not recognised,** from rest: a touch wakes the lock, so it wakes exactly as waking does, the blur, the dim, the clock's lift and the field on waking's own durations and curves. The field arrives already showing the miss: its placeholder says Finger not recognised, and from `short` on the reader's mark eases from `primary` to `error` over `short` on `standard` as the field settles. With it, from `short` on, the mark alone shakes at half the wrong password's jolt (one and a half `space.s4`, back one, a half, each over `micro` on `standard`, then home over `short` on `standard_decel`), a whole device pixel at a time. The field doesn't move and its tube stays as it was.
- With reduce motion nothing pushes or slides, at rest or in any move: the lock holds the desktop's 1:1 picture with the city over it and no plate, and each move is a fade over `short`: locking fades the desktop out as the rest clock and its kicker fade in behind the city; waking fades the awake lock in (the field and the clock at its awake step, the blur and the dim over the picture and the city); going back to rest fades it out over `short` and the rest clock in behind the city over the next `short`; a finger not recognised wakes as waking does, the reader's mark already in `error`; unlocking fades the lock to the desktop; and neither the field nor the reader's mark shakes.

Keys: at rest, any key on the screen with the pointer wakes it, a press too, and a finger the reader doesn't know, with its miss shown; a known finger unlocks it from rest. A key that types a character lands in the field as its first. Awake, Enter checks what is typed; Esc empties the field and goes back to rest; the field is the only stop for Tab; typing goes into the field whatever had the keyboard, and each key, press or touch keeps it awake for another `duration.lock_idle` (20 s), after which it goes back to rest. Waking lands the keyboard in the field; going to rest lands it on the lock, so the next key wakes it; unlocking gives it back to what had it before.

*Not built yet: no QML draws the clock or its kicker from the screen's size, or stands it at the screen's share of its height; the proportional clock and its one place are the mockup's alone.*

# Lock

Part of the closed set of components: [DESIGN.md, "Components"](../../DESIGN.md#components) has the rules every component keeps, its variant words and the fixed measures the geometry check holds it to.

## `Lock`

QML `Lock`, a template of the shell's alone: only the shell locks, so it is in `KubaOS.Components` only, with its section on the QML specimen's "Shell only" page. Its mockup page is the source of truth: Templates, Lock, in `design/mockups/`. *Not built yet: `modules/lock/LockSurface.qml` draws the lock as a column over a band of `opacity.backdrop`, with the clock in `clock` and the `size.password` field under it, in the light or dark scheme of the mode.*

The lock screen. It has two phases, rest and awake, and always draws in the night scheme matugen derives from its wallpaper (`scheme-fidelity` in dark, as `dot-theme` makes it, with its derived roles), whatever the light or dark mode, so it looks the same in both.

**At rest** it is the wallpaper and its clock, and nothing else: no frame, no bar, no field. Every screen of a setup shows rest, each with its own clock.

- **The clock** is the digits in `type.clock_rest` (384, Geist Light, about 280 tall) in the wallpaper's own colour, under a kicker line `space.s16` above the digits' tops: where and which day in `clock_place` (東京・月曜日, M PLUS 1), then ` · ` and the date in `clock_date` (Geist), both 20 on a 24 line, medium, in one line. The digits' box is their cap height down to their baseline. The kicker and the digits are each centred on the clock's middle, on whole device pixels, the odd one after. With a 12-hour clock the digits carry no meridiem; it takes its own slot at the kicker's end (· PM), so the digits keep one size. The figures are Geist's own, proportional, so the digits' box takes each time's own width and stays centred on the clock's middle as a minute changes it.
- **The digits' colour** is a light tint of the picture behind them, worked out from the picture as the shell loads it, so they read as part of it rather than as words laid on it. Of the picture's pixels under the digits' box, each pixel's OKLCH hue counts by its chroma squared, so a sky's glow and a street's signs decide it and grey haze and dark walls barely do; the chroma is theirs, weighted the same way. That hue, at that chroma times 1.2 and at lightness 0.84, fitted into sRGB by lowering its chroma alone, is the colour (Shinjuku's pink horizon gives a pale pink, Tokyo Tower's blue hour a pale blue, Shibuya's lit storefronts a gold). The digits are large enough to read without a contrast of their own, so they hold none and nothing shades them; until the picture has arrived they are `on_surface`. The kicker takes the same colour, its date at `opacity.clock_date` (0.85) of it, so where and which day lead the line.
- **Its place** is the wallpaper's: each wallpaper names the middle of the digits' baseline as shares of the picture's width and height, so the clock keeps its place against the city however a screen crops the picture (`object-fit: cover`), held `space.s32` inside the screen's edges. A picture with no place of its own (yours) puts it in the middle, a little above.
- **The cut-out**, where the wallpaper has one, is its foreground cut out (an alpha picture of the city, the wallpaper's size), lying exactly over the picture and in front of the clock, so the towers stand in front of the digits. The place covers part of the digits and leaves them readable (Shinjuku's skyline covers their feet; Tokyo Tower's spire crosses the last digit) and never covers the kicker. A picture without one has the clock in front of it.
- **The kicker's halo** is all that stands it off the picture, and no shape: round each letter two text shadows of `surface`, a tight one blurred by `size.halo_edge` (1) at `opacity.halo_edge` (0.50) that holds the strokes' edges and a soft one blurred by `size.halo` (6) at `opacity.halo` (0.35) round the word. They follow the glyphs, so no capsule or band shows, and they are the same on every wallpaper, never worked out from the picture: the clock matching its wallpaper matters more than the kicker's contrast. Measured round each letter at rest, the kicker holds about 2.5:1 on Shinjuku's pink sky, 4.4 to 5.4:1 on Tokyo Tower's blue hour, and on Shibuya 12:1 for the place over a dark awning while the date crosses lit signs, 3.8:1 at its middle and under 2:1 at its brightest. The digits have no halo, with a cut-out or without.

**Awake** is the screen with the pointer only; the others stay at rest.

- **The frame** closes in round the picture: `surface` at `opacity.frame`, `space.s8` all round, the top edge as thin as the others, its inner corners at `radius.surface`. Its top edge grows to `size.bar` only while unlocking, into the desktop's bar.
- **The picture** blurs to `size.lock_blur` (8) and dims under `scrim` at `opacity.panel_scrim`, as a panel's desktop does; the blur holds its edges, so it reaches the screen's.
- **The clock** lifts out from behind the cut-out, which fades, and settles at `type.clock` (80), centred, the kicker's top `space.s48` under the frame's top edge. Its digits go from the picture's tint to `on_surface` as it lifts, as the frame's words and the bar's time are, and back as it settles. The kicker keeps its size and goes to its own roles with them, where and which day in `primary` and the date in `on_surface_variant` at full opacity, over the frame and the blurred, dimmed picture as the digits are; its halo stays and is lost in the dim. It holds 4.3:1 or more round each letter there on all three wallpapers.
- **The sheet** is a `Sheet` on the middle of the bottom edge, curving into the frame through concave fillets of `radius.surface`, `size.panel` (384) wide, with a PanelPage's padding: `space.s16` at its top and foot, the container's padding at its sides. It holds the md `Field` for the password (`lock` leading in the field's icon colour, Password as its placeholder, lit while the keyboard is in it); `space.s4` under it, as a field's note is, a line led by `fingerprint` in `primary`, "Or touch the fingerprint reader", in `small`, `on_surface_variant`; and `space.s16` under that the `KeyHints` ↵ Unlock and Esc Rest.
- No avatar, no initial, no emoji: only KubaOS Symbols.

States: checking, the field's symbol is `hourglass_top` and its placeholder Checking…; wrong, the field shakes, empties and turns Invalid, and the line under it says what went wrong in `error` in place of the reader's line, until the next key.

**The desktop's still.** The lock and the desktop are one surface. The session locks in the first frame of Super+L, and the lock captures a still of the desktop then; locking and unlocking play on that still, drawn inside the lock's own surface: the windows, the frame with `size.bar` at the top, and the bar's pills, among them the bar's clock (`modules/bar/Clock.qml`: a `Pill` centred on the bar, the weekday's kanji in `accent`, `tertiary`, the short date in `body`, `on_surface_variant`, and the time in `body`, `on_surface`; the pill eases to its new width as a minute changes the time's, as every pill does). The clock flies between the bar's time and its own place, above the frame while it flies and behind the cut-out once it stands, its digits `on_surface` in the bar and the picture's tint at rest. It lands on the bar's time on whole device pixels, its left edge, its width, its baseline and `type.body`'s size. Geist Light's figures are a little narrower than Regular's, so as it lands its letters spread by what the bar's time is wider, an even share after each: it lands as wide as the bar's time, each figure within a fifth of a logical pixel of the bar's. The two are one family and differ only in weight, Light and Regular, so the handover is the bar's Regular fading in over a `micro` on top of the landed Light, which then goes, and leaving is the same backwards: the strokes thicken or thin in place and nothing else moves. The session unlocks as the unlocking ends, and the real windows take the still's place.

Motion:

- **Locking (Super+L):** the windows fade and shrink to `scale.behind` (0.96) round the middle of the screen over `medium` on `emphasized_accel`, the bar's pills fading out with them. The bar's time hands over to the lock's clock where it stands over `micro`; from `micro` on the clock grows out of the bar to `type.clock_rest` at its rest place over `long` on `emphasized_decel`, its digits taking the picture's tint as it goes, the kicker fading in above it over `short` from `micro` and `medium` on. Meanwhile, from `micro` on, the frame's top edge thins from `size.bar` to `space.s8` on the same curve, and from `short` on the whole frame fades out to the bare picture over `long` on `emphasized_decel`. Once the clock has landed it drops behind the picture and the cut-out fades in over its digits over `short`: the city steps in front. With no cut-out it drops behind nothing. A lid close or an idle suspend skips the motion and goes straight to rest. Until the picture has arrived the screen is `surface_container_lowest`.
- **Waking:** the frame closes in and the picture blurs over `long` on `emphasized_decel`, the dim comes over `medium` on `standard`, the cut-out fades over `short` on `standard`, the clock lifts over `long` on `emphasized_decel`, its digits going from `type.clock_rest` to `type.clock` on whole device pixels, and the sheet rises as a Sheet opens.
- **Going back to rest:** the sheet closes as a Sheet does and the frame opens out, both over `medium` on `emphasized_accel`; the dim lifts over `medium` on `standard`, the blur clears over `long` on `standard`, the clock settles back over `long` on `emphasized_decel`, and the cut-out fades back over `short` from `medium` on, as the clock lands. The field empties.
- **Unlocking with the password,** from awake, about 0.8 s in all: the field's `lock` turns to `lock_open` in `primary` over `short`. The sheet sinks into the bottom edge as a Sheet closes, over `medium` on `emphasized_accel`, from `short` on. From `short` on too, the clock flies from `type.clock` into the bar's clock over `long` on `emphasized_decel`, its kicker fading over `short`, while the frame's top edge grows from `space.s8` into the bar and the bar's pills fade in, on the same curve; once it has landed it hands over to the bar's time over `micro`. From `medium` on the picture's blur and dim clear to the desktop's wallpaper and the windows settle in from `scale.behind`, over `long`.
- **Unlocking with the fingerprint,** from rest: the cut-out and the kicker fade over `micro`, so the clock rises above the frame before it reaches the frame's top edge, and the rest clock flies straight from `type.clock_rest` into the bar's clock over `long` on `emphasized_decel`, its digits going from the picture's tint to `on_surface`, and hands over to the bar's time over `micro` once it has landed. The frame closes in from past the screen's edges with its top edge growing into the bar and the bar's pills fading in, on the same curve, and from `short` on the windows settle in from `scale.behind` over `long`.
- **A wrong password** shakes the field: `space.s12`, back `space.s8`, `space.s4`, each over `micro` on `standard`, then home over `short` on `standard_decel`.
- With reduce motion each move is a fade over `short`: locking fades the desktop out as the rest clock, its kicker and the cut-out fade in; waking fades the awake lock in (the frame, the sheet and the clock in their awake places; the cut-out gone at once); going back to rest fades it out over `short` and the rest clock and the cut-out in over the next `short`; unlocking fades the lock to the desktop; and the field doesn't shake.

Keys: at rest, any key on the screen with the pointer wakes it, a press too, and a touch of the reader, and a key that types a character lands in the field as its first. Awake, Enter checks what is typed; Esc empties the field and goes back to rest; the field is the only stop for Tab; typing goes into the field whatever had the keyboard, and each key, press or touch keeps it awake for another `duration.lock_idle` (20 s), after which it goes back to rest. Waking lands the keyboard in the field; going to rest lands it on the lock, so the next key wakes it; unlocking gives it back to what had it before.
